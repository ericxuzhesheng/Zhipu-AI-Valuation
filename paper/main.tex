\begin{abstract}
\noindent Technical progress can attract a market revaluation before a foundation-model laboratory generates sustainable cash flow. We examine this gap for Zhipu AI using disclosures through 23~October~2026 (Beijing time) and prices through 23~October. Scenario DCF, reverse DCF and layered comparables describe the operating outcomes implicit in its valuation; an event study examines returns around five official GLM releases. H1 revenue grew 399.7\%, but operating loss reached RMB2.15bn and operating cash outflow RMB3.37bn. The scenario-weighted equity value is HK\$116 per share against a HK\$499.0 close, with a substantial contribution from pro-forma net cash. Holding the model's other assumptions fixed, the quoted price requires about US\$35.9bn of revenue in 2035. The five releases have a 13.7\% mean two-day reaction, rising to 15.3\% relative to MiniMax. A block bootstrap supports an unusually large immediate relative response within this sampling design; subsequent drift remains statistically inconclusive and sensitive to the benchmark. Zhipu fell 46.4\% during September, although its relative performance improved in the final week. These observations show that the initial re-rating did not provide a durable valuation floor. Overlapping news prevents attribution of the decline to a single cause. Completed financing, ZCode's data-handling controversy and competing developer tools identify commercial channels through which technical capability may affect shareholder cash flow. An illustrative Anthropic IPO model applies the same distinction between revenue expectations and cash-flow value at a different scale. The analysis establishes conditional valuation gaps and short-run responses, rather than a causal test of mispricing or statistical mean reversion.

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} foundation models; Chapter 18C; pre-profit valuation; real options; market
efficiency; capability news; commercialisation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Zhipu AI (2513.HK) and MiniMax (00100.HK) listed in Hong Kong on 8 and 9~January~2026. Zhipu used Chapter~18C, the regime for specialist-technology firms that admits pre-profit issuers. By 23~October its shares were 4.3 times the IPO price, despite falling from HK\$1,195 at the end of August to HK\$499.0. Its post-placement equity value of US\$31.2bn represented 148.9$\times$ LTM revenue of US\$209.5m through 2026H1 \citep{tushare2026,prospectus2513,prospectus100,placement2513}. This combination of rapid appreciation, a large subsequent decline and continuing losses raises a valuation question: what must technical progress achieve commercially to support the public-market price?

The paper addresses three related questions. How do investors respond around official model releases? What revenue, margins and investment requirements are consistent with the current price? How do the open-weight strategy, developer adoption and access to capital affect those assumptions? A release can improve a laboratory's technical position without establishing the volume of paid usage, customer retention or eventual cash flow. Financial disclosures remain informative for a loss-making firm, even when earnings multiples offer little guidance.

Model releases provide information events alongside the earnings announcements studied by \citet{ball1968} and \citet{bernard1989}. We adapt the reaction and drift windows in the surprise-event design of Guosen Securities \citep{guosen2020} to official GLM releases. The empirical variable is the dated announcement: we do not independently measure how far each release exceeded investors' prior expectations. ``Capability surprise'' in the title denotes this research motivation, rather than an estimated surprise score. Combining return patterns with scenario and reverse DCF connects the market response to technical news with the commercial outcomes required by the valuation.

Information is reviewed through 23~October~2026 (Beijing time), with prices through 23~October. US daily bars use each exchange's local trading date; the Friday US close occurs after the Beijing information cutoff. The two cutoffs are recorded separately. The original five-event sample remains fixed at 31~August; September and October extensions are reported separately. Sections~\ref{sec:bg} and \ref{sec:context} establish the operating and financing context. Section~\ref{sec:val} measures conditional valuation gaps, Section~\ref{sec:event} examines the release windows, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} considers the later repricing and an Anthropic comparison.

\section{Background: Company, Financials, and Listing}
\label{sec:bg}
Founded in 2019 out of Tsinghua University's Knowledge Engineering Group, Zhipu develops the GLM (``General Language Model'') family. It earns revenue from usage-based API access, enterprise subscriptions and private or on-premise deployment. Releasing model weights and charging competitive token prices can encourage developer adoption; the commercial objective is to convert that adoption into paid usage through Z.ai and Z.code. Whether this produces attractive unit economics is an empirical question.

The H1 results show progress in adoption alongside substantial operating costs. Revenue rose 399.7\% to RMB953.9m; open-platform and API revenue reached RMB825.2m, or 86.5\% of the total. API gross margin improved from $-0.4\%$ to 24.6\%, while the shift toward this business reduced total gross margin from 50.0\% to 26.4\%. Operating loss widened 13.0\% to RMB2.15bn, and adjusted net loss widened 12.1\% to RMB1.96bn. Reported net loss narrowed partly because losses on investor financial instruments fell after listing. Underlying losses remained substantial despite the revenue increase \citep{interim2026}.

At 30~June the group had positive equity of RMB4.43bn, bank cash and short-term investments of RMB4.50bn, and bank loans plus leases of RMB2.60bn. The formal interim report discloses H1 operating cash outflow of RMB3,374.1m, versus RMB1,327.2m a year earlier. Note~14 identifies RMB106.0m of restricted deposits, leaving cash equivalents of RMB3,887.7m \citep{interimreport2026}. The August announcement and September report have different information dates even though they describe the same reporting period. Their financial content informs the valuation; the later cash-flow disclosure cannot retrospectively explain earlier returns. Appendix~\ref{sec:statements} reproduces the statements and cash reconciliation.

Zhipu is an early-commercial-stage, pre-profit business. Revenue growth, API gross margin, operating expenditure and funding capacity therefore provide more useful anchors than an earnings multiple (Figure~\ref{fig:fin}). Positive API gross margin is a necessary step toward commercial viability, but it does not cover the laboratory's research and other operating costs.

Zhipu priced its IPO at HK\$116.20, raising about HK\$5.0bn after the over-allotment option, with HK\$3bn of cornerstone commitments and an implied valuation near US\$6.7--7.4bn \citep{prospectus2513,annualreport2513}. Chapter~18C provided access to public equity before profitability. The subsequent re-rating also made follow-on financing possible: on 13~July the company placed 19.78m new shares at HK\$1,588, raising HK\$31.375bn net and increasing issued shares from 445.843m to 465.623m \citep{placement2513}. Further September financing enters the equity bridge in Section~\ref{sec:val}.

At the 23~October close Zhipu remained approximately 329\% above its IPO price, with realised annualised volatility near 179\%. MiniMax, which listed at HK\$165, closed at HK\$187.6. These prices show how substantially market assessments can change during an early public trading history. They do not isolate the effects of model releases, financial results or investor flows.

\section{Industry, Macro, and Strategy}
\label{sec:context}
The foundation-model business depends on costly compute and competes with both closed and open-weight alternatives. Compute-export restrictions can constrain supply; open weights can make alternative models easier to evaluate. Enterprise integration and data-handling requirements may still make switching costly, so low technical switching barriers should not be equated with low customer switching costs in every deployment.

Zhipu competes on coding performance, model access and price. GLM-4.6 used approximately 355B parameters, 32B active parameters and a 200K context. GLM-5.1 reported a 58.4\% SWE-Bench Pro score and a first-place ranking; GLM-5.2 introduced MIT open weights and a one-million-token context in June. GLM-5.3 followed on 14~August and GLM-5.3-Flash on 26~August. The GLM-5.x releases also brought API price increases of 8--17\% \citep{glm_modelcard,glm53,glm53flash}. These product facts motivate the dated events in Section~\ref{sec:event}. They do not independently measure investors' surprise or prove a persistent pricing advantage. Branch releases such as GLM-5V-Turbo are catalogued but excluded when they overlap a main GLM window \citep{glm5v}.

The open-weight approach can broaden adoption while also increasing competition from accessible alternatives. Higher token prices, lower inference costs and greater paid volume could improve gross profit, but research spending and compute dependence determine how much reaches operating cash flow. The H1 mix and margin changes show why technical benchmarks and revenue growth alone cannot settle the valuation question.

\textbf{Market access and investor flows.} Chinese AI policy and state or SOE demand form part of the demand environment, while compute-export controls constrain available infrastructure. Hong Kong index membership and Stock Connect access also affect the potential investor base. Zhipu joined the Hang Seng Tech Index on 5~June and Southbound Stock Connect on 8~June, with about HK\$920m of net southbound buying on its first eligible day \citep{hsi2026}. MSCI added its H shares to the Emerging Markets and China indexes at the 31~August close \citep{msci2026}. The event catalogue distinguishes these dates from model and financial announcements. Classification helps interpret the news; it does not prevent their return windows from overlapping.

\section{Valuation}
\label{sec:val}

\subsection{Cost of capital}
Zhipu's short post-IPO history makes a regression beta unstable. We therefore estimate 60-month monthly betas for global AI-software comparables against SPY through 31~August, unlever them, and round the median of 1.55 to 1.6 \citep{nasdaqsec2026}. With a 4.0\% risk-free rate and 6.0\% equity risk premium, the CAPM calculation gives $4.0\%+1.6\times6.0\%=13.6\%$. We retain 13.5\% as the FCFF discount-rate benchmark. This is a modelling convention based on comparable operating risk, not a fully estimated WACC for Zhipu's current capital structure. Mature software peers may understate the risk of a pre-profit laboratory, and the fixed rate does not capture changes in operating or financing risk through 2035. Appendix~\ref{sec:beta} records the bridge; sensitivity analysis examines alternative rates.

The model uses HK\$7.8/US\$, RMB7.1/US\$ and 487.588m shares after the September placement. It adds US\$6.293bn of pro-forma net cash and short-term investments to operating enterprise value. This bridge combines June cash and investments, less restricted deposits, bank loans and leases, with July and September placement net proceeds and September bond net proceeds, less an issue-date bond-principal proxy \citep{interim2026,placement2513,zhipucompletion2026}. It is a financing-adjusted bridge, not a reported September cash balance. Unreported H2 operating cash consumption would reduce the cash available to shareholders.

The 18~September completion announcement confirms that 21,965,000 new H shares were placed at HK\$714 on 16~September, raising HK\$15,664.13m net. RMB20.14bn of US-dollar-settled zero-coupon convertible bonds were issued on 18~September, raising US\$3,010.77m net \citep{zhipucompletion2026}. The placement's 9.96\% discount was measured against the 11~September HK\$793 close. Initial conversion is at HK\$892.50; maturity is 16~September~2027, and the ordinary conversion period begins on 1~July~2027 \citep{zhipufinancing2026}.

The bond claim is represented by US\$3,000.64m, calculated as disclosed US\$3,015.64m gross proceeds divided by the 100.5\% issue price. This proxy is not an IFRS fair-value estimate; dollar settlement depends on the bond terms and exchange rates. We do not separately estimate the conversion option. The non-conversion case uses 487.588m shares and deducts the bond claim. Full conversion at the initial price would increase shares to 513.953m and remove that claim; applying both dilution and the debt deduction would double-count it. Zero coupon still leaves an economic financing cost and a near-term repayment or refinancing obligation. The proposed allocation of 60\% of net proceeds to GLM development and infrastructure links the financing to future operating execution.

\subsection{Scenario DCF}
We project FCFF over 2026--2035 from US\$700m of FY2026E revenue. This is an analyst estimate, not company guidance. Starting from reported H1 revenue of RMB953.9m, it implies approximately RMB4.02bn in H2. That increase is demanding, although below the August run-rate reported by management after the results \citep{interim2026,caixin2026}. Operating margin begins at $-100\%$, already a sharp improvement from H1's approximately $-225\%$, and rises toward a scenario-specific terminal level. Revenue growth fades from each scenario's 2027 assumption.

The model uses a 15\% tax rate, a sales-to-capital ratio of 1.6--2.0 for reinvestment and 4\% terminal growth. Negative EBIT generates a simplified NOL carryforward; cash taxes arise only after positive EBIT absorbs it. The loss treatment and terminal extrapolation are simplifying assumptions, rather than a detailed statutory tax forecast. Scenario weights express the analyst's judgement and are not probabilities estimated from market prices. Appendix~\ref{sec:proj} gives the Base-case projection and its terminal-value limitation.

The scenario-weighted equity value is HK\$116 per share, or 23.3\% of the market price. Most of its US\$7.256bn equity value comes from the US\$6.293bn pro-forma net-cash bridge; the weighted operating enterprise value is approximately US\$0.963bn. Funding capacity therefore contributes materially to these equity estimates. Raising capital changes the cash and share-count bridge without by itself improving operating profitability.

The difference from market price is conditional on the assumed revenue path, margins, investment requirements, discount rate and financing bridge. These demanding operating assumptions still produce a much smaller equity value than the quote. This result identifies the commercial scale required by the valuation; it does not establish an objective price to which the stock must return.

\subsection{Reverse DCF: what the price requires}
We invert the model to ask what justifies the US\$31.2bn equity value while holding FY2026E revenue fixed.
At WACC~13.5\% and a 40\% terminal margin, the price requires \textbf{about US\$35.9bn of 2035 revenue}:
2027 growth of roughly 108.6\%, followed by a path that still compounds at 54.9\% from 2026 to 2035.
Figure~\ref{fig:revheat} grids WACC (10--20\%) against terminal margin (20--50\%). Even the most favourable
cell (10\%/50\%) requires about US\$10.6bn, while 15\%/40\% requires US\$51.4bn. The reverse DCF is therefore
a description of the revenue path needed under specified assumptions. Different margins, reinvestment requirements or business models would imply different paths.

\subsection{Relative valuation}
\label{sec:rel}
We divide each listed lab's 23~October equity value by LTM revenue through 2026H1. Zhipu's RMB~1{,}487.3m
(FY2025 less H1~2025 plus H1~2026), or US\$209.5m at RMB7.1/US\$, gives \textbf{148.9$\times$}.
MiniMax's US\$8.40bn equity value and US\$165.2m LTM revenue give \textbf{50.9$\times$}. Private transactions imply
\textbf{34.1$\times$} for OpenAI, \textbf{20.5$\times$} for Anthropic, and \textbf{39.0$\times$} for Mistral
\citep{minimax2026h1,openai2026,anthropic2026,mistral2025}. This private range, with a 34.1$\times$ median, is
the football-field transaction range. Applying these transaction multiples to Zhipu's FY2026E revenue estimate gives HK\$230--437 per share. MiniMax's LTM multiple applied to Zhipu's LTM revenue gives HK\$170.4.

MiniMax's 26~August results anchor the peer denominator. H1 revenue rose 283.1\% to US\$116.6m and gross margin
reached 17.9\%, while adjusted net loss doubled to US\$293.0m. Its US\$165.2m LTM revenue equals FY2025 less
H1~2025 plus H1~2026, rather than annualised H1 revenue \citep{minimax2026h1}.

SenseTime, Phancy, and Wenge are Hong Kong adjacent-AI references; Palantir, Cloudflare, and Snowflake represent
commercialisation \citep{hkcomps2026,softwarecomps2026}. Different economics make both groups context only.
On the matched LTM basis, Zhipu's multiple is 2.93 times MiniMax's
(Figure~\ref{fig:comps}).

The multiple comparisons require different degrees of caution. MiniMax provides the closest listed laboratory and a matched revenue period, but its product mix, customers and margins still differ. Private financing valuations use transaction-specific terms and revenue run-rates from different dates; applying them to estimated FY2026 revenue is an illustrative conversion, not a matched public-market valuation. The public and private ranges therefore remain separate.

Wenge illustrates the importance of business mix. Its H1 revenue of RMB172.750m was 65.9\% deployment and 34.1\% subscription and API, with a 55.0\% gross margin. Adjusted net loss narrowed 29.5\% to RMB52.920m, while R\&D expense grew faster than revenue \citep{wengeh12026}. These economics differ from Zhipu's API-heavy mix, so Wenge is an adjacent reference rather than a direct foundation-laboratory valuation anchor. Appendix~\ref{sec:wenge} records its financial detail and the contribution of fair-value gains to reported losses.

\subsection{Sensitivity and synthesis}
Figure~\ref{fig:tornado} reports one-at-a-time sensitivity around the Base case. The tested settings produce approximately HK\$74--131 per share. None of these individual changes bridges the gap to HK\$499.0, but the test does not cover correlated changes, different business models or higher weights on extreme outcomes.

Figure~\ref{fig:ff} compares the conditional ranges. Scenario DCF spans HK\$56--255; applying the 20.5--39.0$\times$ private-deal multiples to FY2026E revenue gives HK\$230--437. Applying MiniMax's 50.9$\times$ LTM multiple to Zhipu's LTM revenue gives about HK\$170.4, 65.9\% below the close. All three approaches leave a material gap, although their denominators and economic assumptions differ. The September decline has therefore reduced the quote without removing the need for substantial future commercial success. Neither HK\$116 nor a historical average is an empirically established long-run price mean.

\section{Capability Surprise: An Event Study}
\label{sec:event}
\subsection{Design and interpretation}
Under semi-strong efficiency, prices incorporate public information promptly \citep{fama1970}. Model announcements may contain information about future paid usage, pricing power or costs. We examine the immediate return response and subsequent drift, drawing on post-earnings-announcement research \citep{ball1968,bernard1989}. A positive response is consistent with investors revising their assessment; subsequent continuation or reversal alone does not identify underreaction, overreaction or a change in expected fundamental value.

Event dates come from official release announcements and technical documentation, independently of the price series \citep{glm_modelcard,minimax_m3}. Daily prices are drawn from Tushare and Yahoo Finance \citep{tushare2026,yfinance2026}. We use leakage $[-5,-1]$, reaction $[0,+1]$ and drift $[+2,+10]$ windows \citep{mackinlay1997,guosen2020}. Expected return is Zhipu's mean daily return over the 15-session estimation window $[-20,-6]$, so $AR_t=R_t-\overline{R}_{[-20,-6]}$ and $CAR_{[a,b]}=\sum_{t=a}^{b}AR_t$. CAR is an arithmetic sum of abnormal simple returns, not a compounded buy-and-hold return.

The short estimation window is useful for a recently listed firm but can be unstable during large price moves. Official dates avoid selecting events from price spikes; they do not eliminate anticipation, simultaneous announcements or changes in discount rates. The fixed August cohort and the separate September extension preserve this distinction between event selection and later interpretation.

\subsection{Immediate response and subsequent drift}
Across the five GLM releases, mean reaction CAR is $+13.7\%$ and mean drift is $+4.6\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:car}). Four reactions are positive. \subsection{Overlapping news and the peer benchmark}
Two large raw up-days, approximately $+43\%$ on 20~February and $+37\%$ on 13~May, are catalogued as index or flow dates. The February observation also falls inside GLM-5's drift window. Index membership and Stock Connect news can therefore contribute to CARs around a capability announcement; categorising the initiating event does not produce an isolated capability effect. Zhipu's 9.6\% rise on 31~August preceded the after-close H1 results and coincided with the effective MSCI rebalance \citep{msci2026,interim2026}. The H1 disclosure cannot explain a return that occurred before its release.

The expanded catalogue records model releases, financial disclosures, flow catalysts, listings and screened candidates, with reasons for exclusions. Twenty-nine events have complete windows in the historical CAR panel. ``Complete'' describes available observations, not the absence of other news. This catalogue makes sample construction auditable without eliminating confounding events.

We also calculate $AR_t=R_{Zhipu,t}-R_{MiniMax,t}$. With this peer benchmark, the five-event mean reaction rises from $+13.7\%$ to $+15.3\%$, and mean drift from $+4.6\%$ to $+16.8\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:robust}). All five relative drift estimates are positive. GLM-5-Turbo changes from $-19.3\%$ to $+19.2\%$, and GLM-5.1 from $-14.2\%$ to $+12.9\%$. The sign changes show why the mean-adjusted reversals should not be labelled evidence of overreaction.

MiniMax shares some sector and macro exposures with Zhipu, but also has its own product announcements: M2.5 followed GLM-5 by one day, and M2.7 followed GLM-5-Turbo by two. The adjusted estimates measure relative performance against this particular firm, rather than removing all sector shocks. They support relative strength during the focal windows; the bootstrap tests how unusual the sample means are within the observed return history.

\subsection{Block-bootstrap evidence and limits}
With five focal events and volatile returns, inference depends heavily on the sampling design. We draw 20,000 bootstrap samples of five peer-adjusted return blocks, matching the two-day reaction and nine-day drift lengths. Eligible blocks lie outside the five focal GLM $[0,+10]$ windows; they can still contain other financial, product or flow news. The comparison therefore uses the observed trading environment as a reference, not a news-free counterfactual (Figure~\ref{fig:boot}).

The actual mean reaction of $+15.3\%$ exceeds the 95\% null interval of $[-14.0,+12.6]\%$ and lies at the 99.2nd percentile. Mean drift of $+16.8\%$ lies inside the wider $[-22.8,+25.5]\%$ interval, at percentile 88.0. Immediate relative responses are unusually large under this design; evidence for persistent drift is inconclusive. This result does not isolate a causal capability effect, estimate an AGI-success probability or establish a general market-efficiency anomaly. A larger multi-laboratory sample and independently scored surprise magnitudes would be needed for those stronger questions.

\section{September Repricing and Commercial Conversion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Complete supplementary windows}
The September extension provides observations that were incomplete at the original August cutoff. GLM-5.3-Flash records a $+9.1\%$ reaction followed by $-25.9\%$ drift. Zhipu's H1 announcement, mapped to 1~September after its 31~August after-close release, records $-8.1\%$ reaction and $-54.4\%$ drift. MiniMax's H1 announcement records $-5.7\%$ and $-23.2\%$. The Tencent Hy4 and MSCI windows are also complete. These observations remain outside the fixed five-GLM estimates; all are recorded in \texttt{eventstudy/september\_window\_update.csv}.

The Flash, financial-results and index windows overlap. Their negative subsequent CARs describe the repricing episode but are not independent replications of a capability-related reversal. The two companies' financial windows also differ in timing. They cannot identify how much of the decline came from margins, funding expectations, sector demand or other concurrent information.

Over the full calendar period from 31~August to 30~September, Zhipu fell 46.36\% and MiniMax 28.31\%, a difference of 18.05 percentage points. These are compounded raw-price returns, distinct from event CARs. Relative performance also depends on the selected period: from 24 to 30~September, Zhipu gained 0.39\% while MiniMax fell 10.32\%, giving a 10.71-percentage-point spread. The month-end improvement shows that the relative repricing was not monotonic.

From 30~September to 23~October, Zhipu fell 22.15\% to HK\$499.0, while MiniMax fell 25.02\% to HK\$187.6. The 2~October monthly return confirms 263,059,605 H shares and 224,528,485 unlisted shares, totalling 487,588,090. The 1,222,934 awards granted after the 30~September close were unvested and did not add issued shares during September. The 9~October after-close supplement clarifies performance conditions; it cannot explain that day's close \citep{zhipumonthly2026,zhipuawards2026}.

The decline represents a partial unwinding of earlier gains. Interpreting it as a compression of optimistic commercial expectations is consistent with the remaining 148.9$\times$ LTM multiple and the conditional DCF gap, but these methods do not estimate a capability premium as a separate price component. We do not estimate a long-run price mean, test stationarity or demonstrate convergence to DCF value. Commercial costs and financing requirements offer plausible explanations for the changing valuation; overlapping news prevents causal attribution.

\subsection{Developer trust, tooling and incomplete windows}
On 18~September, developer ferstar alleged that ZCode uploaded workspace snapshots including Git history and that settings did not disable the upload path \citep{zcodeanalysis2026}. The analysis was not independently reproduced here. In an evening response reported by IT Home, ZCode apologised, attributed uploads to default-enabled indexing or Repo Wiki, said the issue was fixed and data destroyed after Wiki generation, and promised source release and external review \citep{zcoderesponse2026}. These statements are company claims. ZCode published Apache-2.0 source on 21~September, followed by a GitHub v3.14.3 source release in late September \citep{zcodeopen2026}. It reported removal of Repo Wiki and the snapshot-upload path in v3.14.0, citing CAICT and NSFOCUS reviews \citep{zcodereview2026}. The signed review reports and historical deletion evidence were not independently verified. Available evidence does not establish that every repository was uploaded or used for training.

MiniMax Code CLI v0.4.12 was released under MIT as an inspectable and extensible terminal coding-agent implementation \citep{minimaxcode2026}. It differs from the older general-purpose MiniMax CLI and from model weights. The official desktop changelog separately lists v3.15.1 on 12~October (a random HighSpeed trial and workflow/permission changes) and v3.15.3 on 13~October (update, login and parallel-request fixes) \citep{zcodeoct2026}. These desktop dates must not be conflated with GitHub source-release dates. The two developments illustrate competition for the developer workflow: ZCode seeks to restore confidence in data handling, while public CLI source can make an alternative easier to evaluate. Current source alone does not verify historical or deployed behaviour, and the evidence does not show that MiniMax timed its release in response to ZCode.

Data-control confidence can affect enterprise approval, developer trials, retention and paid usage. Remediation costs or longer procurement cycles could affect Zhipu; MiniMax could gain consideration without capturing revenue. Its CLI supports other models, so tool adoption need not increase MiniMax API consumption. No disclosed migration or revenue-loss estimate supports changing the operating DCF assumptions. The mechanism matters to valuation even while its quantitative effect remains unknown.

At the 23~October cutoff, the 21~September response/CLI window has all nine drift sessions. Zhipu's mean-adjusted reaction is $-0.53\%$ and drift $+16.81\%$; the peer-adjusted figures are $+0.79\%$ and $+10.99\%$. The formal interim-report and preview windows now both have nine drift sessions. Their mean-adjusted drift is respectively $+25.70\%$ and $-15.01\%$ (peer-adjusted $+14.79\%$ and $-10.50\%$). The share-award window now has all nine drift sessions, with mean-adjusted drift of $+6.88\%$ and peer-adjusted drift of $+3.72\%$. These overlapping supplementary events remain outside the original five-event sample. The October promotion period has elapsed, but realised usage and revenue have not been independently established. Updated windows are in \texttt{eventstudy/october\_window\_update.csv}.

The prior 16~October review of the issuer-designated Zhipu IR feed listed the 9~October award supplement as its latest filing. The official GLM-5.3-BF16 repository was created in August; its 15~October modification timestamp does not establish a new model launch \citep{zhipuireview2026,glmrepo2026}. A subsequent direct read returned all 52 issuer-feed records. Client-side date filtering excludes the 26~October entry and leaves 9~October as the latest at the cutoff, with no 17--23~October entry. Titles were garbled, so this date-list check does not establish new document contents. Disclosed shares and the cash bridge are retained. ZCode's official changelog adds v3.16.2 on 20~October and v3.16.3 on 23~October; its security-fix statements have not been independently reproduced. MiniMax's 16~October integrity pledge supplies governance context without a quantified financial change \citep{minimaxpledge2026}. The new A-share tutoring-progress story is media corroboration, not a verified approval or completed financing \citep{minimaxtutoring2026}. The original tutoring document was not obtained. This week the HKEX title search and original 23~October announcement were accessible: MiniMax reported completed technology export registration for certain models, at 22:33 Beijing time after the close. This is not an earnings release or completed financing \citep{minimaxexport2026}. No unsupported proceeds, issued shares or revenue are inserted into the Zhipu model.

MiniMax officially released M3.1 Flash on 19~October, a Hong Kong holiday; the first following observed session is 20~October. Its API pricing is US\$0.15 input and US\$0.60 output per million tokens, with a company-claimed 70\% task-cost reduction \citep{minimaxflash2026}. The 22~October Code Mini App launch adds a persistent application interface \citep{minimaxminiapp2026}. At the cutoff these windows have two and zero observed drift sessions respectively. Date-only launch windows are provisional; no complete nine-session drift or causal release effect is claimed. The after-close export-registration disclosure has no observed post-event session. These rows remain outside the fixed August sample.

\subsection{Capital access and uncertain upside}
A high equity valuation can support research by allowing a firm to raise cash for compute and talent. This financing feedback resembles the reflexivity interpretation discussed by Feng Liu \citep{fengliu}. July's placement at HK\$1,588 and September's at HK\$714 illustrate how financing terms can change as prices move, although their dates, sizes and terms differ. New funding extends investment capacity, while dilution and debt repayment affect how much eventual success accrues to each share. Access to capital is therefore part of the commercial conversion process, not evidence that the process has already succeeded.

Real-options theory offers an analogy for uncertain upside \citep{black1973,damodaran2018}: investors may value opportunities to expand into a much larger market. We do not calculate an option price or infer a unique probability from the gap above DCF. Different growth expectations, risk assessments and model omissions can produce the same gap. The event study measures short-run returns around announcements; it does not observe changes in the perceived probability of an AGI outcome. The reverse DCF makes the required operating scale explicit without choosing between these interpretations.

The current comparison benchmark is US\$1.5tn; the earlier US\$2tn expectation is a historical stress case, and neither is a completed transaction. The completed May funding reference remains in the private-company comparable set \citep{anthropic2026}. The 22~October Reuters report retains the US\$1.5tn discussion and describes a provisional mid-November IPO with marketing in the week of 9~November \citep{anthropictimetable2026}. New product and governance disclosures do not quantify incremental cash flows. Table~\ref{tab:anthropicstatus} separates observations from assumptions.

Anthropic's 18~October Public Benefit Fund announcement proposes a 5\% share commitment subject to shareholder approval, with discretionary additions tied to valuation milestones \citep{anthropicfund2026}. It does not establish completed dilution or justify a mechanical 5\% reduction in enterprise value. Claude Duet entered a US Pro/Max research preview on 21~October; the Anthropic-managed Claude Platform on Google Cloud became generally available on 22~October \citep{anthropicduet2026,anthropiccloud2026}. The 26~October public S-1 announcement is a subsequent event \citep{anthropics1public2026}. It is recorded separately and its financial disclosures do not enter the 23~October model. The zero net-cash assumption and the absence of IPO share-price estimates remain explicit.

Growth then declines to 3\% in 2036, when revenue reaches US\$578.0bn. EBIT margin increases from 10\% to 35\%, and positive EBIT incurs 25\% cash tax. The model assumes D\&A at 3\% and capex at 8\% of revenue. Their net cash investment cost is therefore 5\% of revenue before working capital.

Working capital is a balance equal to 2\% of revenue. Each year's UFCF deducts the increase in that balance, rather than a recurring charge equal to 2\% of revenue. Starting working capital is US\$1.28bn on the assumed US\$64bn 2026 revenue anchor. In 2028, for example, the US\$67bn revenue increase requires US\$1.34bn of additional working capital. The cash-flow bridge is
\[
\mathrm{UFCF}_t=\mathrm{EBIT}_t(1-0.25)+\mathrm{D\&A}_t-\mathrm{Capex}_t-\Delta\mathrm{NWC}_t.
\]

A reverse calculation holds the explicit cash flows, terminal revenue, tax, investment ratios, WACC and terminal growth fixed. Supporting the latest US\$1.5tn discussion benchmark requires a terminal EBIT margin of 79.5\%; the earlier US\$2tn stress case requires 110.8\%. The treatment of compute costs also affects the comparison. Reuters reports cloud-distribution dependence and, in October, at least US\$518bn of decade-long compute commitments \citep{anthropicprospectus2026,anthropicquarter2026}. Payments for rented compute, training and distribution may enter operating expenses and therefore EBIT margin; owned infrastructure may require capex. The same cost should not be deducted in both places. Conversely, fixed margin and capex ratios may understate the timing or scale of committed expenditure. The model does not contain a contract-level expenditure schedule, so it cannot establish that these ratios fully capture the commitments. Long-term compute commitments are not automatically current interest-bearing debt.

Net cash is assumed zero, temporarily equating enterprise and equity value. No IPO proceeds, new debt or fully diluted share count are incorporated because their amounts are not established in this comparison; no per-share IPO price is estimated. The assumed 10$\times$ forward revenue multiple also uses a different denominator from the May funding reference's 20.5$\times$ annualised-revenue multiple, so the difference does not measure observed multiple compression. Drivers, annual cash flows and sensitivities are reproduced in \texttt{scripts/anthropic\_ipo\_crosscheck.py} and its CSV outputs.

The supplied October workbook also separates frontier laboratories, growth software, neoclouds and scale references. We retain these as methodological context rather than pool them into Zhipu's core transaction range. Its 14.66$\times$ frontier median uses two OpenAI marks, not two independent firms; software and infrastructure rows mix dates and forecast periods, and enterprise-value multiples cannot be applied as equity values without a cash/debt bridge. The US\$64bn anchor is a hard-coded analyst estimate motivated by a reported run-rate path; the supplied workbook does not independently derive an annual recognition schedule. These limitations matter more than mechanical agreement among multiples.

For Zhipu, this comparison clarifies why a revenue forecast alone cannot determine shareholder value. The Anthropic and Zhipu models have different valuation dates, taxes, discount rates and reinvestment methods, so their absolute values are not a controlled comparison or an additional Zhipu price target. In each model, valuation depends on how revenue becomes operating profit, how much investment growth requires and how financing affects the claim held by shareholders.

\section{Conclusion and Implications}
The five original GLM release windows show positive average immediate returns, including an unusually large mean response relative to MiniMax under the block-bootstrap design. Evidence for persistent drift is weaker: it remains inside the bootstrap null interval, and individual signs change with the benchmark. September's decline shows that the earlier re-rating did not establish a durable price floor. The sample does not identify a capability-driven pricing error or statistical mean reversion.

The operating evidence makes commercial conversion the central valuation question. H1 revenue grew 399.7\% and API gross margin turned positive, while operating loss exceeded twice revenue and cash outflow increased. Against the HK\$499.0 close, scenario-weighted DCF gives HK\$116, the illustrative private-transaction range HK\$230--437, and the matched MiniMax LTM multiple HK\$170.4. The reverse DCF requires approximately US\$35.9bn of revenue in 2035 under its selected assumptions. These conditional gaps describe how much growth and operating improvement the price requires; they do not provide a proven reversion target.

Financing capacity, developer trust and customer retention connect model quality to future cash flow. Completed placements and convertible issuance provide resources alongside dilution and repayment obligations. ZCode's remediation and competing inspectable tools may affect supplier evaluation, but migration and revenue effects remain unmeasured. The Anthropic comparison reinforces the need to specify margins and investment requirements even when revenue expectations are very large. Further evidence on retained paid usage, unit economics, cash consumption and financing terms would determine whether technical progress becomes durable shareholder value.

The Base case still has US\$4.369bn of NOLs at the end of 2035. Terminal value extrapolates the final forecast FCFF at 4\% growth; it does not separately normalise cash taxes after the loss balance is exhausted. These conventions have opposing valuation effects: zero-tax extrapolation tends to raise value, while retaining the higher investment intensity tends to reduce it. \section{Bottom-Up Beta Bridge}
\label{sec:beta}
The bottom-up beta uses public software and AI-infrastructure comparables with observable market betas and
capital structures. Levered betas use 60 month-end returns through 31~August~2026; market value is that day's
close times the latest disclosed shares, and debt comes from the latest pre-cutoff SEC filing. We unlever as
$\beta_U=\beta_L/[1+(1-T)D/E]$, using a 21\% tax rate, market capitalisation as equity value, and total debt
as debt \citep{nasdaqsec2026}. We round the median unlevered beta of 1.55 to 1.6 as a working reference
for operating risk. No Zhipu-specific re-levering or debt-and-equity-weighted WACC is calculated;
13.5\% remains the common discount-rate assumption used by the valuation scenarios. WACC labels
in the model tables and figures refer to this discount-rate assumption.
